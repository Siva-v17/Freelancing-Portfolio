My story

Started with a beginner IT-services website. Never really stopped building.

My first real project was a simple, commented HTML/CSS/JS site called Stackly — built to learn, not to impress. Since then that same name has grown into an entire ecosystem: fitness platforms, education dashboards, car rental sites, healthcare portals — each one sharpening how I think about layout, state, and motion.

Today I work independently with founders and small teams who want their product to feel considered — not templated. I care about the seams: what happens on a slow connection, what a dashboard looks like with real data, how a button feels when you hover it.

Journey

How the craft was built, one project at a time.

Year 1 — Foundations

Learning by shipping

Built the first version of Stackly as a beginner IT-services site — hand-commented HTML, CSS and vanilla JS, split cleanly across files to actually understand what each line did.

Year 2 — React & state

Moving into product thinking

Built InternMS, a full internship-management platform with custom hooks, context-based global state and protected routing — the first project that needed real architecture decisions.

Year 3 — Motion & systems

GSAP became the default

Started layering ScrollTrigger reveals, animated preloaders and micro-interactions into every build, and began developing a repeatable design-system approach across client sites.

Year 4–5 — Full ecosystems

Multi-product freelance practice

Now building complete branded ecosystems — marketing site, auth flow, admin dashboard and user dashboard — for clients spanning fitness, education, healthcare, logistics and retail.

Principles

How I approach every build.

01

Content first

Real copy and real data before real design — placeholders lie about how a layout will hold up.

02

Mobile is not an afterthought

Every layout gets tested and tuned across breakpoints, not just resized down from desktop.

03

Motion with intent

Animation should clarify hierarchy and guide attention, not perform for its own sake.

04

Ship, then polish

A working version in your hands beats a perfect one still in progress.
